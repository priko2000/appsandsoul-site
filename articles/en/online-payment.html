Online payment in Israel: what the options are

There are several ways to accept payment on a website in Israel. Here is how it works and what to check before signing a contract.

David Mushailov, founder of Apps & Soul

How it works

The website itself does not process cards. Payment processing companies (חברות סליקה) do that. The customer taps “pay”, lands on the company’s secure payment page, and the money reaches your account. Card details are not stored on your site, which is good for security.

Which payment methods can be connected

  • Credit cards, including payment in instalments (תשלומים), which Israeli buyers are used to.
  • bit, Apple Pay and Google Pay — through many Israeli payment companies.
  • PayPal — if you have customers abroad.
  • Cash on delivery or bank transfer — for those who prefer not to pay online.

Note: some built-in payment solutions of popular builders are not available in Israel. For example, Wix itself states that Wix Payments does not work in the country and suggests connecting local companies.

What to compare

  • The fee per transaction. Online it is often higher than in a store because the fraud risk is higher.
  • Monthly fee and setup fee.
  • How long it takes for money to reach your account.
  • Automatic invoices and receipts — they save a lot of time.
  • Contract length and exit terms.

Do you need it right away?

Not always. Many stores start with ordering through the site and payment on delivery or by link, and add online payment once orders grow. What matters is that the site is ready for it from the start.

Payment companies’ terms change; check the current terms with the company itself before signing.
