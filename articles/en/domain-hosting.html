Domain, hosting, email: what they are in plain words

Three words that sound technical but stand for simple things. Once you understand them, nobody can confuse you, and you stay in control of your website.

David Mushailov, founder of Apps & Soul

A domain is the address

What people type into the browser: for example, myshop.co.il. You buy a domain from a registrar and renew it every year. Forget to renew, and someone else can take the address.

  • Businesses in Israel often choose .co.il or .com.
  • Short, easy to say, no hyphens or letters that get confused.
  • Most important: the domain should be registered in your name, not the developer’s.

Hosting is where the site lives

A server that stores the site’s files and shows them to visitors. There is cheap shared hosting and cloud hosting that handles heavy loads. For a small site it is usually inexpensive, and for simple sites it can even be free.

A secure connection (SSL)

The padlock in the address bar. Today it is the bare minimum: without it the browser says “Not secure” and customers leave. It is usually free and set up together with hosting.

Email on your own domain

An address like info@myshop.co.il looks more professional than a regular mailbox. It can be set up so that messages arrive in the inbox you already use.

What to check right now

  • Whose name the domain is registered in and when it renews.
  • Whether you have access to the registrar and hosting accounts.
  • Whether the padlock works on your site.

If you have no answer to one of these, find out now, not on the day the site stops opening.
