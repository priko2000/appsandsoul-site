Work and payment stages: what to look for in a contract

A good contract protects both sides and removes disputes before they appear. You do not need to be a lawyer to check the essentials.

David Mushailov, founder of Apps & Soul

The usual project stages

  1. Conversation and plan. You describe the task and receive a written plan: what is included, timelines, cost.
  2. Mockup. What the site will look like. Design revisions happen here.
  3. Development. The site is built and filled with content.
  4. Review. You go through the site and list your comments.
  5. Launch and training. The site goes live on your domain, and you are shown how to manage it.

Paying in stages

It makes sense when payment is tied to stages: part at the start, part after the mockup is approved, the rest at launch. That way neither side risks the full amount.

What the contract should include

  • Scope of work. A list of pages, features, languages. The more specific, the fewer disputes.
  • Timelines for each stage, and what happens if your materials are delayed.
  • Revisions. How many rounds and up to which stage.
  • Work outside the plan. How it is priced: separately and only with your consent.
  • Rights. Who owns the site, code, design and domain after payment.
  • Warranty. How long after launch bugs are fixed free of charge.
  • Support. What the terms are after the warranty period.

A tip

Put every agreement in writing, even verbal ones, if only as a WhatsApp message. Three months later it will help both of you remember what was agreed.

This article is general and is not legal advice.
