Website accessibility in Israel: what the law requires

Israeli regulations require websites that serve the public to be accessible to people with disabilities. Here are the essentials in plain words.

David Mushailov, founder of Apps & Soul

Where the requirement comes from

It is the Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Law and the 2013 service accessibility regulations. The section on websites (Regulation 35) says that websites and apps through which a business provides a public service, or information about it, must be accessible.

What “accessible” means

The site must meet Israeli Standard 5568, which is based on the international WCAG guidelines, at level AA. In practice that means, for example:

  • the site can be used with a keyboard, without a mouse;
  • images have text descriptions for screen readers;
  • text has enough contrast and can be enlarged without breaking the page;
  • forms, buttons and links are clearly labelled;
  • animations can be stopped.

Accessibility statement

The site must have an “Accessibility statement” page (הצהרת נגישות): what has been done, what has not yet, and how to contact the person responsible for accessibility.

A widget button is not accessibility yet

Many sites add a ready-made button with font and contrast settings and consider the job done. But such a tool alone is not enough: the site itself must be built accessibly in its code.

Exemptions

The law provides exemptions and reduced requirements in some cases, for example when the burden on the business is too heavy. Sometimes an exemption applies automatically, and sometimes it must be requested from the competent authority. Details are on the website of the Commission for Equal Rights of Persons with Disabilities and on the Kol Zchut portal.

Why it is good for business

Beyond the law, it simply means more customers: older people, people with poor eyesight, anyone browsing on the go with one hand. An accessible site is more convenient for everyone.

This article is general and is not legal advice. For your case, consult an accessibility specialist.
