A website, a home-screen app or an App Store app?

Business owners often say “we need an app” while meaning quite different things. In practice there are three options, and they differ a lot in capabilities, timelines and costs. Here is an honest look at each.

David Mushailov, founder of Apps & Soul

1. A regular website

It opens from a link in any browser, on a phone or a computer. People find it on Google, and it is easy to share on WhatsApp. It is the fastest and most affordable option, and for many businesses it is enough.

2. An app on the phone’s home screen

This is the same website, built so the customer can add it to the phone’s home screen. From then on it opens like an app: with its own icon, full screen, without the browser’s address bar.

  • On iPhone you add it through Safari: “Share” → “Add to Home Screen”. On Android the browser offers to install it.
  • There is no app store review, and updates reach customers immediately.
  • It can send notifications, including on iPhone once it has been added to the home screen.

There are limits too: this kind of app is not listed in the App Store or Google Play, and some phone features are unavailable to it or only partly available.

3. A full app in the App Store and Google Play

It is downloaded from an app store. It integrates more deeply with the phone and looks more established to regular customers. But it comes with more costs and rules:

  • A developer account: Apple charges $99 a year, Google a one-time $25.
  • Every version is reviewed by the stores, so updates are not instant.
  • Development and maintenance usually cost more than a website.

Where it makes sense to start

Most small businesses are better off starting with a website that also works as a home-screen app. It is quick, affordable and already gives the customer an icon on their phone. A full app makes sense when you have many regular customers who order often, or you need phone features a website does not have.

The good news: these steps do not exclude each other. You can start with a website and later move to a store app without losing products, orders or customers.
