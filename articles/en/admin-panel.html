A management panel: what the owner sees and changes alone

A website without a convenient management panel is like a store whose keys are held by someone else. Here is what a good panel should contain and why.

David Mushailov, founder of Apps & Soul

What the owner should do alone, without a developer

  • Change prices and stock, add and hide products.
  • Switch promotions on and off.
  • See new orders and update their status.
  • Change opening hours, banners and short texts.

What is useful to see

  • Orders today and this week — how many, for how much, which are still unprocessed.
  • What sells best and what is not moving.
  • What is running out, so you can reorder from the supplier in time.
  • Where customers come from: Google, social media, WhatsApp.

Convenience matters more than the number of buttons

A good panel is not the one with a hundred features but the one where frequent actions take two taps, including on a phone. If changing a price means going through five screens, people stop using the panel.

Permissions and security

  • Different permissions for staff: orders for the seller, reports for the accountant, everything for the owner.
  • An activity log: who changed what.
  • Secure login and regular backups.

It grows with the business

You can start simple: products and orders. Later add stock, couriers, a till connection, reports. What matters is that the foundation allows adding, not starting over.
